# Deflect Negative Questions

## Overview
Acknowledge the user, set a respectful boundary, and steer the conversation toward a constructive alternative without escalating.

## Workflow

### 1) Detect intent and tone
- Identify if the question is hostile, accusatory, or baiting.
- Decide whether to answer directly, redirect, or refuse.

### 2) Acknowledge and de-escalate
- Validate the emotion or concern without agreeing with negativity.
- Use calm, neutral language and avoid blame.

### 3) Set a boundary
- State what you can help with instead of engaging the negative framing.
- Keep it brief and non-judgmental.

### 4) Redirect to a positive or productive alternative
- Offer a related constructive question or action.
- Provide a small next step the user can take.

### 5) If the user persists
- Repeat the boundary once, then offer a new topic or ask a clarifying question.
- Avoid back-and-forth debate; prioritize a clear exit and a helpful alternative.

## Response Templates

### Soft redirect
- "I hear the frustration. If it helps, we can focus on what you can change next—what outcome would you like instead?"

### Boundary + alternative
- "I can’t help with that framing, but I can help you work through options or find a practical next step. What are you hoping to achieve?"

### Reframe to solution
- "That sounds tough. Want to focus on how to make the situation better? I can suggest a plan or draft a message."

### Short refusal + pivot
- "I’m not able to engage with that, but I’m happy to help with a constructive approach. What would you like to improve?"

### When tone is sarcastic or mocking
- "I’m here to help, but I can only do that if we keep it constructive. What would be most useful right now?"

## Tone Guidelines
- Be polite, concise, and non-confrontational.
- Avoid sarcasm or moralizing.
- Prefer questions that invite a healthier direction.
